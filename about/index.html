About

Beyond the research.

Born in Anchorage, Alaska, I currently reside in Bloomington, Indiana. Growing up surrounded by nature, I love to slip away to hike in the woods or just enjoy a walk, often with my Hungarian or Spanish bagpipes.

I started my career in historical linguistics before shifting to folklore studies, where I focused on how people make meaning through musical technology and objects. In parallel with my academic pursuits, I followed a second career as an analyst and researcher in the translation and localization space. I was involved in the development of many of the foundational standards for interoperability when I worked for the Localization Industry Standards Association (LISA). After LISA ceased operation, I worked for the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI) in Berlin on projects related to quality. Since 2016 I have been back in the U.S.

I enjoy teach and sharing information with others and have recently take up outreach work to help language professionals recognize scams that target them. I also informally mentor graduate students at Indiana University to help them navigate the challenges of graduate school.

If people know one thing about me other than my professional life, it is that I am a passionate musician on various and sundry instruments, many of them odd of uncommon. I am currently working on some projects to record large-scale works on the hurdy-gurdy.

01

Musical performance & instrument building

I play hurdy-gurdy, various types of bagpipes, whistles, and other strange things.

Since I was a child, I have been fascinated by the technology of musical instruments and the skill of performing on them. I started on piano at age five and then learned to play double bass. But I really came into my own when I discovered the hurdy-gurdy (I have five in my house at present). My wife tells me that I don’t truly own an instrument until I have disassembled it and improve some aspect, and I have gotten into instrument building and repair as well as using 3D printing to prototype new instruments. I also do outreach to children and adults to teach them about music.

In addition, I am a member of the Indiana Slavic Choir, led by Iryna Voloshyna, and the Columbus, Indiana Philharmonic Chorus. In the Slavic Choir, we perform folk music from around the Slavic and Central/Eastern world, with an emphasis on Ukrainian polyphonic singing. I enjoy the closeness of performing in small groups with others where we have to listen carefully to one another. On the opposite scale, I have sung in works as monumental as Beethoven’s 9th (insane for singers, but if you surrender to the insanity an absolute thrill) and Verdi’s Requiem.

02

18th-century music, reenactment & clothing

A few years back I was drafted to play music for a reenactment. Because I wanted the clothing right, that led me down a rabbit hole of hand sewing all my attire. I love the way that hand-sewing forces me to slow down and focus on the process, but I end up with something I can wear that I made. The tactile nature of the task helps bring back into connection with making and doing things.

Making historical garments or learning period music requires shifting out of the familiar to try to inhabit the mind of another in places both similar and different from our own. Making clothing like breeches is not for the faint of heart as so many things can go wrong, while articles like shirts are much easier to make.

03

Bowtiks: My small boutique line of men’s neckwear

For many years I have made and sold men’s bow ties

I work almost exclusively with batiks, which provide such an assortment of colors and contrasts to play with. Most of my ties have contrasting colors on each side, which allows the wearer to choose the blade color and then have either the same color for the knot or the contrasting one. But no matter how you tie them, a bit of the other side pops through. Just recently I started to revive my line after a hiatus of several years.

04

Hiking & long-distance walking

In 1997, my younger brother and I walked 475 miles on the Camino Frances, one of the Ways of St. James. This started my passion for long-distance hiking. Unfortunately, life has intervened and I have yet to hike the Pacific Crest Trail: I’d only need about six months. Funnily enough, no employer wants to give me the time off.

When we hiked the Camino, only about 15% as many people hiked it as now. Back in the days before most of us had cell phones and social media was a thing of the future, we had nothing to do but walk and think. I loved the awareness of the land around me that grew and developed over time.
