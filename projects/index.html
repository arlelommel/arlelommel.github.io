01

Global Revenue Forecaster

Market opportunity · quantitative modeling · decision support

A forecasting system designed to help organizations estimate how much additional revenue they could potentially realize by improving support for international markets and languages.

The Global Revenue Forecaster translates research on language, market behavior, digital access, and company performance into a practical planning tool. Its purpose is not simply to rank markets, but to help organizations reason about where additional multilingual investment may create measurable commercial opportunity.

The project combines external market data with company-specific inputs so that results reflect the scale and circumstances of an individual organization rather than a generic global ranking.

As a project, it illustrates a recurring theme in my work at CSA Research: taking analytical findings that would otherwise remain in reports or datasets and turning them into a reusable system that supports concrete business decisions.

02

Global CX Calculator — B2B

Customer experience · language strategy · B2B

A decision-support tool for evaluating the relationship between language support, customer experience, and commercial opportunity in business-to-business markets.

The B2B Global CX Calculator is intended to help organizations evaluate where language and localization choices affect the experience of business customers across markets.

It brings together research findings and structured data in a form that allows users to explore market differences rather than treating language support as a simple yes-or-no requirement.

The project is designed around a practical question: given limited resources, where will improvements in multilingual customer experience matter most for a particular business?

03

Global CX Calculator — B2C

Customer experience · language strategy · B2C

A companion system focused on consumer markets, where language preferences, digital behavior, population characteristics, and market scale interact differently than they do in B2B settings.

The B2C version of the Global CX Calculator applies the same general research-to-tool approach to consumer-facing organizations, where the relevant populations and decision criteria differ substantially from business markets.

It is designed to make complex multilingual market data usable for planning by allowing organizations to compare countries, languages, and customer populations in a consistent analytical framework.

Keeping the B2B and B2C systems distinct makes it possible to model the assumptions and evidence appropriate to each rather than forcing both into a single generic view of global customer experience.

04

Global Website Analysis

3,000+ brand websites · language coverage · localization depth

A large-scale analysis of how global brands support languages and locales across more than 3,000 websites, designed to turn public web behavior into comparable evidence about multilingual strategy.

The project measures which languages and locales companies support and how deeply they localize their digital presence. The aim is to move beyond anecdotal comparisons and create a consistent basis for comparing companies within and across industries.

At its current scale, the analysis covers more than 3,000 brand websites. The resulting data can support company-level reports as well as industry-level benchmarking, including comparisons of language coverage and localization depth among peer organizations.

The project combines web-scale data collection, multilingual analysis, normalization across inconsistent site structures, and visualization intended for both research and client-facing use.

Localization Industry Sizing

Bayesian modeling · market estimation · methodology redesign

A replacement for an older market-sizing approach that attempts to infer changes in the localization industry from changes in the revenues of individual companies.

CSA Research has historically estimated changes in the size of the localization industry using a model built around observed changes in company revenues. That approach is being replaced because company-level revenue movements are an imperfect proxy for changes in the market as a whole and do not adequately represent the uncertainty in the available evidence.

The new work uses a Bayesian approach so that multiple forms of evidence can be combined explicitly and uncertainty can be represented rather than hidden behind a single deterministic estimate.

The project is therefore as much a methodology redesign as a market-sizing exercise: It starts by identifying where the older model makes claims stronger than the underlying evidence can support, then builds a framework intended to produce more defensible estimates as new information becomes available.

Integration of Research and Client Data

Generative AI · proprietary research · client-specific analysis

An applied GenAI project that combines CSA Research's accumulated research catalog with customer-specific information to produce analysis grounded in both sources.

The project explores how generative AI can make a large proprietary research catalog more useful when it is combined with data and context supplied by an individual client.

Rather than treating a language model as a general-purpose source of answers, the system is intended to use CSA’s existing research as the analytical foundation and bring client information into that context so that outputs are specific to the customer’s situation.

This creates a bridge between a traditional research business, in which findings are published as reports, and an interactive model in which accumulated research can be applied dynamically to a client’s own data and questions.

07

New Advent Corpus

Open scholarly resource · corpus development · TEI

An open corpus of public-domain English translations of early Christian writings, prepared for computational and scholarly use.

The New Advent Corpus grew out of academic work on a large collection of public-domain translations of writings by the early Church Fathers. The source texts had been gathered and maintained by another compiler; with permission, I prepared them for computational use through segmentation and conversion into TEI.

The result is a reusable scholarly dataset rather than a one-off research artifact. It preserves the underlying collection while adding structure that makes the material more useful for corpus analysis and other forms of computational research.

The project also reflects a separate strand of my work from commercial research: building open resources that other researchers can inspect, reuse, and extend.
